How to Find a Clothing Manufacturer in 2026

9 min read · Updated June 2026

Finding the right clothing manufacturer is the single most important decision for a new apparel brand. This step-by-step guide shows you where to look, how to vet factories, what to ask, and the red flags that signal you should walk away.

Home / Blog / How to Find a Clothing Manufacturer

The manufacturer you choose shapes your product quality, margins, timelines and ultimately whether customers come back. Yet most founders rush this decision, pick the first factory that replies, and pay for it later in delays, quality issues and blown budgets. This guide walks through a structured process for finding and vetting a clothing manufacturer so you start your brand on solid ground.

Step 1: Define Exactly What You Need

Before contacting any factory, get clear on your product. The more specific you are, the better quotes and matches you'll get. Document:

  • Product type — hoodies, t-shirts, activewear, denim, etc. Factories specialize, and a great knitwear factory may be poor at woven outerwear.
  • Quantity — your realistic first-order volume per style and color.
  • Quality tier — fast-fashion basics versus premium heavyweight pieces.
  • Decoration — sublimation, screen print, embroidery, DTF.
  • Budget & target retail — your landed cost ceiling per unit.
  • Branding — custom labels, tags and packaging needs.

If you can, prepare a basic tech pack — a one-page spec with measurements, materials, construction notes and artwork. It dramatically improves quote accuracy and reduces sampling errors.

Step 2: Decide Domestic vs Overseas

Domestic manufacturers offer faster shipping, easier communication and simpler quality oversight, but usually at higher unit costs and often with higher minimums. Overseas manufacturers typically provide lower costs, broader capabilities and lower MOQs, with the trade-off of longer shipping and time-zone coordination. Many modern manufacturers — including FABRIOZA — bridge the gap with factory-direct overseas production, low minimums, English-speaking sales support and worldwide DDP shipping that handles duties for you.

Step 3: Where to Actually Find Manufacturers

  • Direct manufacturer websites — searching terms like "custom hoodie manufacturer" or "private label clothing manufacturer" surfaces factories that sell directly, cutting out agents.
  • B2B marketplaces — useful for breadth, but many listings are trading companies, not factories.
  • Industry directories and trade shows — strong for vetting and seeing samples in person.
  • Referrals — other founders are the most reliable source of honest factory feedback.

A key distinction: a factory makes the garments, while a trading company resells another factory's production with a 15–30% markup. Factory-direct relationships mean better pricing and clearer communication about what's actually possible.

Step 4: Vet Every Candidate

Shortlist three to five manufacturers and evaluate them on more than price:

  • Certifications — ISO 9001 for quality management signals consistent processes. Look also for OEKO-TEX, BSCI and WRAP depending on your priorities.
  • Specialization — do they regularly make your product type?
  • MOQ fit — can they meet your realistic first-order size? A 50-piece minimum is far friendlier to new brands than 500.
  • Communication — fast, clear, detailed replies predict a smoother project.
  • Quality control process — ask how they inspect. A documented multi-stage process with AQL sampling is a strong sign.
  • References and reviews — request examples and talk to past clients where possible.

Step 5: Always Order a Sample First

Never place a bulk order without approving a physical sample. A sample reveals fabric quality, fit, construction and print accuracy in a way photos never can. Good manufacturers produce samples in about 5–7 days and credit the sample cost toward your bulk order. Treat sampling as your single most important quality checkpoint — and as a test of how the factory communicates and solves problems.

Step 6: Understand Pricing and Terms

Get quotes broken down clearly: unit price at your quantity, sample cost, decoration costs, packaging, shipping method (DDP vs FOB) and payment terms. Be wary of quotes far below the others — unusually cheap pricing often means lower fabric quality or hidden costs that appear later. Clarify lead times for both samples and bulk, and confirm what happens if quality falls short.

Red Flags to Walk Away From

  • Vague or evasive answers about MOQ, certifications or QC.
  • Refusal to provide a sample before bulk.
  • Pricing dramatically lower than every other quote.
  • Poor, slow or inconsistent communication during quoting — it only gets worse in production.
  • No verifiable certifications or references.
  • Pressure to skip sampling or commit to large minimums immediately.

Questions to Ask Before You Commit

  1. What is your MOQ per style and per color?
  2. Are you a factory or a trading company?
  3. What certifications do you hold (ISO 9001, OEKO-TEX, BSCI, WRAP)?
  4. What are your sample and bulk lead times?
  5. How do you handle quality control and defects?
  6. Can you provide custom labels, tags and packaging?
  7. What are your payment terms and shipping options?
