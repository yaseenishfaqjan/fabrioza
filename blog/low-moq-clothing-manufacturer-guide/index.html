Low MOQ Clothing Manufacturer Guide

8 min read · Updated June 2026

MOQ — minimum order quantity — is the number one thing standing between an idea and a real clothing brand. This guide explains what MOQ is, why low minimums matter, typical MOQs by product, and how to launch with as few as 50 pieces.

If you've researched clothing manufacturers, you've hit the same wall every founder does: minimum order quantities. A factory quotes a beautiful product, then mentions you need to order 500 units per color to start. For a new brand, that's thousands of dollars locked into unproven designs. Understanding MOQ — and finding a manufacturer with low minimums — is the key to launching without that risk.

What Is MOQ?

MOQ, or minimum order quantity, is the smallest number of units a manufacturer will produce in a single order. It usually applies per style, per color — so a 100-piece MOQ on a hoodie in three colors can mean 300 pieces total. MOQs exist because setup costs (patterns, fabric sourcing, machine setup, decoration screens) are spread across the run; the larger the run, the lower the per-unit cost.

Why MOQ Matters So Much for Startups

High minimums create three problems for new brands:

  • Capital risk — a 500-piece order can mean $5,000–$15,000 tied up before a single sale.
  • Inventory risk — if a design doesn't sell, you're stuck storing and discounting dead stock.
  • Limited experimentation — high minimums force you to bet big on a few styles instead of testing several.

Low-MOQ manufacturing flips this. A 50-piece minimum lets you launch several styles for the cost of one large order, learn what your customers actually want, and reinvest in winners. It turns brand-building from one big gamble into a series of smaller, smarter bets.

Why Do Low-MOQ Manufacturers Cost a Bit More Per Unit?

It's simple math: fixed setup costs spread across 50 units cost more per piece than across 500. Expect to pay a modest premium per unit at low quantities. But that premium is almost always cheaper than the alternative — ordering 500 units, selling 150, and discounting or writing off the rest. For most startups, low MOQ is the lower total-risk path, even at a slightly higher unit price. As you grow, volume discounts (often up to 40%) bring your per-unit cost down.

How to Get the Best Terms

  • Consolidate styles with one factory — ordering several styles together can unlock better pricing even at low per-style quantities.
  • Keep colorways focused — since MOQ is per color, launching two strong colors beats spreading across five.
  • Order a sample first — protects you from committing 50 units to a flawed design.
  • Plan reorders — discuss volume-discount tiers up front so you know your costs as you scale.
  • Choose factory-direct — avoids the trading-company markup stacked on top of low-MOQ pricing.
